Ontology of funerary artefacts at the Egyptian Museum in Cairo

How do you make a machine not only store information about museum objects, but also reason about them?

Period
Nov. – Dec. 2025
Context
Semantic web, Sorbonne Université
Team
Ezéchiel Sawadogo, Mamadou Diouhé Barry, Faycal Salhi, Alexiane Arphexad, Victoria Eyharabide
Tools
Protégé, Pellet, owlready2

The idea

An OWL 2 ontology of the royal sarcophagi and coffins of the Egyptian Museum in Cairo: the deceased, archaeologists, tombs, dynasties, materials, museums and exhibitions. It does more than describe: from the deceased alone, the reasoner infers a coffin's dynasty, historical period, royal status and current location (city, country, continent).

What was implemented

  • Property chains, restrictions, defined classes and disjointness axioms.
  • SWRL rules: automatic dating of objects, detection of pieces kept outside their country of origin (the sarcophagus of Seti I is in London), restoration priorities, visitor fees.
  • Modelling exceptions in a formalism that does not allow them: Hatshepsut, both queen and pharaoh; stone sarcophagus versus gold coffin; the open-world assumption.
  • Consistency checking: a data-entry error (a “wooden sarcophagus”) is detected and explained by the reasoner.
  • Reasoning with Pellet, 11 SPARQL queries, and a Python script (owlready2) that reproduces every inference.
